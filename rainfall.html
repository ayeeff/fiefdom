2025: Asia's Wettest Year

In 2025, the hydrological history of Asia was rewritten. Preliminary global data confirms that every single WMO sub-region across the continent has surged past the precipitation maximums recorded between 1961 and 2020.

This widespread deluge was driven by a rare atmospheric convergence: a weak-to-moderate La Niña coupled with a strong negative Indian-Ocean Dipole—a synchronization seen only three times since 1950. These forces, supercharged by record-warm sea-surface temperatures (>1.5°C above pre-industrial levels), injected critical levels of moisture into every storm system.

From the 19 typhoons that battered the Philippines—including a record-breaking six storms in 30 days—to intensified monsoon surges across the Indian Ocean and Western Pacific, this map visualizes the year the rains simply did not stop.
